# CS-250_Course
Portfolio with projects and research done in software development lifecycle course

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user is trying to accomplish and then translating those needs into requirements that can be developed and tested. User stories helps me with this because they describe a feature and items from the users' perspective instead of only focusing on the technical aspects. During the SNHU Travel project, user stories helped the team identify features such as filtering vacation packages by price, region, and vacation type. Acceptance criteria also helped make the requirements clearer and gave the developers and testers a way to determine when the feature was done.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by breaking larger problems into small portions that can be completed and tested individually. One of the biggest lessons I learned from the Agile processes is that the development plan should be adaptable for when new information or requirements are introduced. In my future development projects, I would like to use practices such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, backlog prioritization, and Sprint Reviews. I would also use estimation techniques such as Planning Poker because its an interesting way for team member to give feedback.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating with the team, completing your responsibilities, and the ability to adapt when they're changes in the project. A developer may really depend on key information from the Product Owner or feedback from the tester before they are able to complete their work. This highlights how important communication is because one team member's work can affect the progress of the rest of the team. Agile practices such as Daily Scrums and Sprint Reviews help create transparency and harmony, so problems can be identified early. A good team member is also willing to listen to other ideas, help solve problems, and contribute toward the team's goal.
